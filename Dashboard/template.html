Standings

Playoff Picture 10,000 SIMULATED SEASONS + PLAYOFFS

Every remaining regular-season game is simulated with the power ratings, then the playoffs are played out under the NHL format (top three in each division plus two wild cards per conference). Each series goes to the stronger team by the backtested series model: power rating plus elite skaters (top-10% talent at the position). Only a team's first two elite skaters count: in past series each of the first two moved the odds a lot and more added nothing measurable, and counting only two gave the most accurate probabilities of every version tested. Playoff strength = chance of beating an average playoff team in a seven-game series, with its 80% range given how unsure we are about the team's true strength. Odds after round one are deliberately modest: the playoffs are close to a coin flip series by series, and even the favorite usually doesn't win the Cup.

Value vs. Contract

Two views. In his role: what he's expected to deliver in the minutes he actually plays (talent times ice time). This is what the team is getting for the money. Raw talent: how good he is at a standard workload. Both are compared with what that cap hit buys on the open market across the NHL. A gap between the two means role, not talent, is driving his value.

Goaltending

Player Finder EVERY NHL PLAYER, SAME MODEL

Every NHL player who has appeared this season, plus every team's rostered players who haven't played yet (marked "no games", rated from past seasons), rated exactly the same way. NHL caliber = talent percentile at his position: Superstar (top 2%), Elite (top 10%), Top of lineup (top third), Middle (middle third), Depth (bottom third), Fringe (bottom 10%). Each step is a real one: a player at the top of a group beats one at the top of the next group down about 80% of the time. Goalies are different: what matters is clearing a floor, not climbing a ladder. Each goalie is compared with the league's 32 likely starters: Below starter floor (bottom 20% of starters; such teams lost about 14 goals a season beyond what their skaters predict, in 10 of 12 seasons tested), Borderline starter (20th-40th) or Starter quality (above the 40th percentile; beyond this, more goalie talent added nothing measurable in the regular season or the playoffs). Goalie ratings are noisy, so treat goalies near a line as uncertain. Filter by team, conference, division, position or age to rank players within that group.
